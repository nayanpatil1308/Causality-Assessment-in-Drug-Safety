# Causality-Assessment-in-Drug-Safety
## 1. Introduction
Causality assessment is the process of evaluating the relationship between a suspected drug and an adverse drug reaction (ADR). It helps determine how likely it is that a medicine caused a particular unwanted reaction in a patient.
In pharmacovigilance, causality assessment is important for identifying potential drug safety risks and supporting the safe use of medicines.

## 3. Important Factors in Causality Assessment
1. **Time relationship:** Did the reaction occur after the suspected medicine was started?
2. **Dechallenge:** Did the reaction improve after the medicine was stopped or its dose was reduced?
3. **Rechallenge:** Did the reaction return after the medicine was restarted? This is not routinely recommended when it could put the patient at risk.
4. **Alternative causes:** Could the reaction be explained by the patient's disease, another medicine, or another condition?
5. **Previous evidence:** Is the reaction already known to be associated with the suspected medicine?
6. **Clinical evidence:** Do the symptoms, laboratory results, and medical records support a possible drug-related reaction?
## 4. Common Causality Assessment Methods
### A. WHO–UMC Method
The World Health Organization–Uppsala Monitoring Centre method classifies suspected drug reactions into categories such as:
- Certain
- Probable/Likely
- Possible
- Unlikely
- Conditional/Unclassified
- Unassessable/Unclassifiable
### B. Naranjo Algorithm
The Naranjo algorithm is a questionnaire-based method that uses questions about timing, previous reports, dechallenge, rechallenge, alternative causes, and other evidence.
Its usual score interpretation is:
- Definite: 9 or more
- Probable: 5–8
- Possible: 1–4
- Doubtful: 0 or less
The score should be calculated from documented evidence, not assumptions.
## 5. Steps in Causality Assessment
1. Identify and document the suspected adverse reaction.
2. Record the suspected medicine, dose, route, and treatment dates.
3. Establish when the medicine was started and when the reaction appeared.
4. Review the patient's medical history and other medicines.
5. Evaluate dechallenge and rechallenge information, if available.
6. Consider alternative explanations and relevant clinical findings.
7. Apply an appropriate causality assessment method.
8. Document the conclusion, supporting evidence, and any limitations.
## 6. Example of a Hypothetical Case
**Case description:** A patient develops a skin rash after starting a prescribed medicine.
- Suspected medicine: Medicine A
- Reaction: Skin rash
- Timing: The rash appeared after the medicine was started.
- Dechallenge: The available records indicate whether the rash improved after discontinuation.
- Alternative causes: Other medicines, infections, and underlying conditions need to be considered.
**Assessment:** The temporal relationship raises suspicion of a possible drug-related reaction. However, a final causality category cannot be assigned without sufficient clinical information and a structured assessment.
## 7. Importance of Causality Assessment
- Supports the evaluation of suspected ADRs.
- Helps identify potential medicine-related safety problems.
- Contributes to pharmacovigilance signal detection.
- Supports evidence-based regulatory and clinical decisions.
- Promotes safer medicine use and patient care.
